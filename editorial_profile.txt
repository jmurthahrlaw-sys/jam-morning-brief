JAM MORNING BRIEF — EDITORIAL PROFILE
Version 4.0

PURPOSE
Create one concise personal morning briefing with two deliberately separate editorial tracks:

A. GENERAL NEWS: the most important national, global, Minnesota, technology, entertainment, and good-news stories.
B. PROFESSIONAL LEGAL NOTES: employment and labor law developments useful to an employment attorney with Minnesota and California practice interests.

These tracks MUST be selected separately. Legal material must not crowd out general news, and unrelated court news must not enter the professional legal section merely because it is “legal.”

FINAL ORDER
1. TOP NATIONAL — exactly 3
2. TOP GLOBAL — exactly 3
3. MINNESOTA — exactly 2
4. EMPLOYMENT & LABOR LAW NOTES — flexible, usually 4–8 when warranted
5. TECH & AI — 1 or 2
6. ENTERTAINMENT & CULTURE — 1 or 2
7. GOOD NEWS — 1 or 2
8. END. No “What to Watch Today.”

GENERAL NEWS EDITORIAL RULES
- Choose stories for a generally informed reader, NOT through an employment-law lens.
- Do not add artificial “employer implications” to ordinary political, economic, disaster, global, Minnesota, tech, entertainment, or good-news stories.
- “Why it matters” should explain the ordinary public significance of the story.
- A story may appear in only ONE general-news section.
- Do not reuse a Global story as the Good News story.
- Prefer consequential reporting over novelty, virality, or volume of coverage.

TOP NATIONAL — EXACTLY 3
Choose the three most consequential U.S. developments.
Possible subjects: federal government, politics, economy, national policy, major business developments, major national events, nationally significant regulation or courts.
A foreign-company story does not become “National” merely because it involves jobs or business.

TOP GLOBAL — EXACTLY 3
Choose the three most consequential international developments.
Prioritize wars/security, diplomacy, elections/government changes, major economic developments, disasters, and consequential international policy/human-rights developments.

MINNESOTA — EXACTLY 2
Choose the two Minnesota developments most worth knowing that morning.
Possible subjects: state government/politics, Twin Cities, economy, education, public safety, infrastructure, environment/land use, major business/community news.

TECH & AI — 1 OR 2
Choose genuinely useful or interesting developments involving AI, consumer tech, cybersecurity, privacy, social platforms, major product/company developments, or meaningful technology policy.
If the central significance is an employer legal obligation, place it in Legal Notes instead.

ENTERTAINMENT & CULTURE — 1 OR 2
Choose genuinely interesting film, TV, streaming, music, books, tours, awards, entertainment-industry, or worthwhile pop-culture news.
This section may be fun. Avoid thin celebrity quote stories and trivial rumor/clickbait.

GOOD NEWS — 1 OR 2
End on genuinely positive reporting: human kindness, community achievement, animal rescue/conservation, scientific/medical progress, recovery/resilience, charitable action, environmental restoration, or remarkable achievement.
Avoid corporate PR disguised as good news.
Never duplicate a story already used in National, Global, Minnesota, Tech, or Entertainment.

EMPLOYMENT & LABOR LAW NOTES — PRIMARY PROFESSIONAL SECTION
This is NOT a general court-news section.

PRIMARY SPECIALIST SOURCES
Review these especially carefully:
1. Lexology Daily Newsfeed
2. Employment Law Information Network (ELINfonet) Daily Employment Law Update

Then prefer:
3. Primary opinions, statutes, regulations, agency releases/guidance
4. Established employment/labor publications
5. High-quality employment-law firm analysis
6. Reuters/AP/major press reporting on an employment-law development

SUBSTANTIVE SCOPE — INCLUDE
Federal:
- EEOC
- NLRB
- U.S. Department of Labor
- OSHA when workplace/employment related
- wage/hour and overtime
- discrimination, harassment, retaliation
- ADA/accommodations
- FMLA and other leave
- pregnancy/lactation protections
- worker classification/independent contractors
- unions and collective bargaining
- restrictive covenants/noncompetes
- federal contractor employment obligations
- workplace AI/automated decision tools
- employment privacy/data
- federal employment/labor statutes, regulations, rules and guidance

U.S. Supreme Court:
- employment/labor cases automatically
- other cases only if unusually consequential to employer regulation, administrative-law authority, civil rights, arbitration, or employment practice

Minnesota federal:
- D. Minnesota employment/labor
- Eighth Circuit employment/labor

California federal:
- Ninth Circuit employment/labor
- N.D. Cal., E.D. Cal., C.D. Cal., S.D. Cal. employment/labor

Minnesota state:
- Minnesota Supreme Court/Court of Appeals employment/labor
- Legislature
- DLI
- Minnesota Department of Human Rights
- wage/hour, leave, discrimination, accommodation, labor, classification, workplace rules

California state — VERY HIGH PRIORITY:
- California Supreme Court/Courts of Appeal employment/labor
- Legislature
- CRD
- DIR/DLSE
- Cal/OSHA
- wage/hour
- leaves/accommodations
- discrimination/harassment/retaliation
- PAGA
- classification
- labor relations
- restrictive covenants
- workplace AI
- privacy
- nonprofit-employer compliance
- employer notices/postings/training requirements

SUBSTANTIVE SCOPE — EXCLUDE
Do NOT include merely because a court issued a decision:
- criminal cases
- eminent domain
- tax-refund procedure
- general pension/government disputes unless materially an employment-benefits issue with practical employer relevance
- unrelated constitutional disputes
- ordinary commercial litigation
- unrelated environmental/land-use matters
- generic HR career advice
- management lifestyle pieces
- law-firm promotional content with no meaningful legal development

HARD RULE
If the development is not substantially related to employment, labor, workplace regulation, employer compliance, employee rights, labor relations, or a genuinely consequential Supreme Court/administrative-law issue affecting employment practice, EXCLUDE IT FROM LEGAL NOTES.

LEGAL NOTE FORMAT
For a case:
- heading: actual case name + court when supplied
- jurisdiction/topic
- development/holding
- employer/practice takeaway
- date when supplied
- source

For statute/regulation/agency action/guidance:
- heading
- jurisdiction/topic
- development
- employer/practice takeaway
- effective/compliance date when supplied
- source

LEGAL ACCURACY
- Never invent a case name, holding, effective date, compliance deadline, or employer obligation.
- Never call a district-court ruling precedent.
- Never describe a circuit ruling as binding nationwide.
- Never combine unrelated cases into one note.
- Do not infer a broad holding from a thin headline.
- If source material is too thin for a confident takeaway, say so briefly or omit the takeaway.
- Do not fabricate an “employer takeaway” where the employment connection is incidental.

LEGAL LENGTH
Usually 4–8 notes on an active day; fewer is better than filler.
A meaningful specialist-source item should not be omitted just because a general-news story is more popular.

WRITING STYLE
- Clear, concise, legally precise.
- General sections: ordinary intelligent news analysis.
- Legal notes: attorney-oriented, practical and denser.
- Avoid canned phrases such as “this highlights the importance of.”
- No sensationalism.
- No “What to Watch.”
- The Good News item is the closing note of the briefing.
