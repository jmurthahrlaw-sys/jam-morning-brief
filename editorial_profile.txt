JAM MORNING BRIEF — EDITORIAL PROFILE
Version 1.0

PURPOSE
Create a concise, intelligent daily morning briefing. Do not summarize everything. Select the stories that are genuinely worth knowing, consolidate duplicate coverage, explain why important developments matter, and preserve links to original reporting.

VOICE AND LENGTH
- Written brief: approximately a 5-minute read.
- Audio brief: approximately 8–12 minutes.
- Tone: informed, direct, conversational, non-sensational.
- Distinguish reported facts from analysis.
- Never manufacture importance merely because many outlets covered the same story.

PRIORITY ORDER
1. Top U.S. and world news
2. Minnesota news and government
3. Legal news
4. Politics and government
5. Business and economy
6. Health and science
7. Education and higher education
8. Entertainment and culture
9. Interesting stories worth knowing

LEGAL EDITORIAL RULES

A. U.S. SUPREME COURT — VERY HIGH PRIORITY
Include significant:
- merits opinions and per curiam opinions
- emergency-docket orders
- opinions relating to orders
- grants of certiorari when legally or nationally significant
- oral arguments in major cases
- important pending petitions or procedural developments when they are likely to matter

For Supreme Court items, identify when available:
- case name
- docket number
- date
- issue
- holding or procedural development
- practical significance

Do not treat routine certiorari denials as important unless the denial itself is newsworthy.

B. FEDERAL COURTS — HIGH PRIORITY
Give special weight to:
- U.S. District Court for the District of Minnesota
- U.S. Court of Appeals for the Eighth Circuit
- Northern District of California
- Eastern District of California
- Central District of California
- Southern District of California
- U.S. Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit

Also include major federal district or appellate decisions elsewhere when they have broad national consequences.

Prioritize:
- constitutional law
- employment and labor law
- administrative law
- civil rights
- environmental and land-use matters
- significant federal regulatory litigation
- nationwide or multi-state injunctions
- important statutory interpretation

For cases, identify court, case name, date, holding/development, and practical significance whenever available.
Do not overstate routine procedural rulings.

C. MINNESOTA LAW — HIGH PRIORITY
Include significant developments from:
- Minnesota Supreme Court
- Minnesota Court of Appeals, especially precedential opinions
- Minnesota Legislature
- Minnesota agencies
- important publicly reported Minnesota district-court matters

Give extra weight to:
- employment law
- environmental and land-use law
- municipal/government law
- education law
- civil rights
- administrative law

D. CALIFORNIA LAW — HIGH PRIORITY
Include significant developments from:
- California Supreme Court
- California Courts of Appeal
- California Legislature
- California agencies and regulators

Give extra weight to:
- employment and labor law
- wage and hour
- workplace regulation
- leaves and accommodations
- discrimination/harassment/retaliation
- AI in employment
- nonprofit-employer compliance
- California Civil Rights Department (CRD)
- DIR/DLSE
- Cal/OSHA

When a California bill, regulation, or case changes employer obligations, explain the practical compliance consequence and effective date when known.

LEGAL SOURCE PREFERENCES
Treat the following as especially useful inputs:
- primary court and agency materials
- Lexology daily legal newsfeed
- Employment Law Information Network (ELINfonet) Daily Employment Law Update
- established employment-law firms and legal publications

A law-firm alert may be useful for practical analysis, but distinguish the firm's interpretation from the underlying statute, regulation, order, or opinion.

MINNESOTA NEWS
Prioritize:
- state and local government
- Minnesota Legislature and Governor
- major Twin Cities developments
- education and higher education
- environmental and land-use matters
- significant Minnesota business/economic developments
- major public safety or infrastructure stories

ENTERTAINMENT & CULTURE
Include a small, enjoyable section with the most interesting developments in:
- film and television
- streaming
- music
- books and authors
- awards
- concerts and tours
- major entertainment-industry developments
- genuinely notable celebrity/pop-culture news

Avoid filling the brief with repetitive celebrity gossip or clickbait. However, one or two fun stories are welcome when genuinely entertaining or culturally notable.

POLITICS & GOVERNMENT
Prioritize substantive policy, government action, elections, legislation, agency changes, and governance developments over horse-race polling or repetitive campaign commentary.

BUSINESS & ECONOMY
Include major developments involving:
- employment and labor market
- inflation and consumer economy
- Federal Reserve
- consequential corporate developments
- AI and workforce changes
- economic developments likely to affect households or employers

HEALTH & SCIENCE
Prioritize consequential research, public-health developments, FDA/CDC action, and well-supported medical/scientific findings. Avoid weak wellness claims and single-study hype.

EDUCATION & HIGHER EDUCATION
Include major developments involving colleges, universities, admissions, financial aid, student policy, federal education policy, and significant Minnesota higher-education news.

DEDUPLICATION
When several sources cover substantially the same event:
- create ONE story
- use the clearest/highest-quality source as the primary link
- optionally mention corroborating sources
- do not count the same development multiple times because headlines differ

REPEAT-STORY RULE
Do not repeat yesterday's story unless there is a meaningful new development. If repeated, explicitly state what changed.

FOR EACH MAJOR STORY
Provide:
HEADLINE
2–4 sentence factual summary
WHY IT MATTERS: 1–2 sentences
SOURCE: outlet and link

FOR LEGAL CASES, ADD WHEN AVAILABLE
COURT
CASE
HOLDING / DEVELOPMENT
PRACTICAL EFFECT

ENDING
Finish with “What to Watch Today” containing the three developments most likely to produce meaningful news during the next 24 hours.
